## How to play Slap Battles

The main loop is simple: equip a glove in the lobby, enter an arena and hit other players to earn Slaps. The Slap total unlocks more gloves, and each glove changes how you approach a fight through its power, speed or special ability.

On PC, click to slap. Some glove abilities are passive, while active abilities normally use `E`. Mobile players receive an on-screen ability button. The official experience recommends shift lock because keeping the camera aligned makes it easier to judge hit direction and the edge of the platform.

Do not try to memorize every glove before entering the arena. Start by learning the range, knockback and recovery of the equipped glove. Those three details matter more than its visual effect.

## A safe first-session route

1. Equip an available glove and read whether its ability is passive or activated.
2. Turn on shift lock if the platform supports it.
3. Enter the normal arena and spend the first few fights learning slap range.
4. Stay closer to the middle until you understand how far opponents can launch you.
5. Test one newly unlocked glove at a time instead of switching after every defeat.
6. Set the next glove as a progression target and earn Slaps through normal play.

Slap Battles contains many badge and event challenges, but they are easier after the movement and camera become automatic. A difficult secret route is a poor place to learn basic spacing.

## Positioning tips for earning more Slaps

**Keep the safer side of the opponent.** Try to stand closer to the centre than the player you are attacking. If both players land a hit, that position gives you more platform behind you and sends the opponent toward the edge.

**Reset after a successful slap.** One hit does not require a long chase. Re-centre the camera, check for a third player and approach again instead of running into the void or another glove ability.

**Avoid the largest crowd.** A busy group offers targets but also creates attacks from several directions. Work around the outside of a fight and hit players who have just committed to someone else.

**Watch the glove, not only the avatar.** An unusual animation often signals an active ability. Give it space once, learn what it does and then decide whether to interrupt, dodge or wait it out.

**Use ability downtime.** When an opponent spends a major ability and misses, move in before it is available again. Do not waste your own ability merely because the button is ready.

## How to practise a new glove

Use three short rounds to answer three questions. First, how close must the slap be to connect? Second, does the ability start pressure, defend or finish a target? Third, how vulnerable are you after it misses?

A lower-cost glove you understand can outperform a rarer glove used without a plan. Passive gloves reward positioning automatically, while active gloves require deliberate timing. If a glove feels weak, test it away from a crowd before deciding it is unusable.

## Surviving chaotic arena fights

Keep the camera wide enough to see nearby players and avoid standing still after an exchange. Jumping constantly can also make movement predictable, so mix grounded strafing with occasional jumps rather than repeating one escape pattern.

When two opponents are already fighting, wait for one to swing before entering. The attack animation creates a safer opening and reduces the chance that both immediately turn toward you. After landing a hit, leave if the position becomes crowded; survival creates more opportunities than one reckless extra slap.

## Slaps and Null Shards are different

Slaps are the main progression count used to unlock gloves. Null Shards are a separate currency associated with special content and rewards.

That distinction helps identify bad cross-game data. A list offering Lucky Spins is describing **Slap**, not Slap Battles. A real Slap Battles reward should match systems that actually exist in the official experience.
