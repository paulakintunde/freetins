## How to play Mugen

Mugen is a fast combat game built around styles, defensive timing and short bursts of movement. Start with the tutorial in the lobby before joining a serious fight. The tutorial introduces the combat loop and currently gives its own completion rewards, so skipping it means losing both practice and an early progression boost.

On PC, hold the left mouse button to attack, hold `F` to block, press `Q` to dash and press `T` to scan for nearby players. When the parry bar is full, tap `F` instead of holding it to attempt a parry. Mobile and controller layouts can change, so follow the action labels shown by the live interface.

The basic rhythm is attack, watch the opponent's response, then defend or reposition. Holding forward and repeating attacks makes your approach predictable. A short pause can invite an opponent to swing first, creating room for a block, parry or side dash.

## A useful first-session plan

1. Complete the lobby tutorial and test every control it introduces.
2. Use one style long enough to learn its range before spending heavily on more spins.
3. Practise blocking normal attacks before trying to parry every exchange.
4. Enter Infinity mode solo to learn enemy patterns, or bring friends when survival matters more than practice.
5. Join a boss attempt only after you can move, block and recover without looking at the controls.

The official experience description says bosses appear on a repeating timer and that weekend boss-drop chances are higher. Treat the live lobby timer as the authority because event schedules can change between builds.

## Mugen combat tips that save runs

**Dash with a reason.** Use `Q` to leave an attack line, close a confirmed gap or escape after a blocked sequence. Repeated forward dashes spend movement without changing the angle.

**Do not hold block forever.** Blocking is safer than taking a full sequence, but a patient opponent can wait for the defensive meter to weaken. Release, reposition and rebuild space when the pressure ends.

**Parry the readable hit.** A full parry bar does not make every tap safe. Look for a committed opening attack or a repeated pattern instead of pressing `F` at random.

**Use Scan before wandering.** `T` helps find players. It is more useful than crossing the map blindly, especially when you are trying to choose a fight rather than enter one by accident.

**Learn the style before judging it.** A rarer style is not a substitute for knowing its range, recovery and safest opener. Save currency until you understand what the current style cannot do.

## Boss and Infinity mode preparation

Bosses punish the same habits as players but make mistakes more expensive. Keep enough distance to see an attack begin, move sideways rather than backing into another hazard and save a dash for recovery. In a group, spread out so one area attack does not hit everyone and let the targeted player focus on survival while the others deal damage.

Infinity mode is also a useful control test. Early waves reveal whether your build can manage repeated enemies, while later pressure exposes wasteful dashes and attacks. A shorter clean run teaches more than a longer run carried by random ability use.
