## How to play Jump Stars

Jump Stars is a fast arena fighter where each character has a different attack pattern, Super Attack and gadget. Choose a first fighter, enter matches and learn that fighter's normal attack range before trying to build the whole roster.

The current PC controls listed by the developer are:

- `Q`: dash
- Left mouse button: normal attack
- `F`: Super Attack
- `E`: gadget

Mobile and controller players should use the matching live prompts because button placement can vary. The important sequence is the same on every platform: position with movement and dashes, land normal attacks, then use the Super Attack or gadget when it can change the exchange.

## How progression works

Matches award resources used for different jobs. Gems are used to summon new fighters. Yen and StarPoints upgrade characters, while trophies track competitive progress. Keeping those currencies separate prevents a common beginner mistake: expecting summon currency to improve a fighter that is already owned.

Focus early upgrades on one or two fighters you can use comfortably. Spreading Yen and StarPoints across every new pull produces a larger roster but can leave every option underdeveloped. Once the main fighter handles matches reliably, build a second choice that covers a different range or play style.

## A practical beginner route

1. Pick one fighter and play several matches without changing characters.
2. Learn the maximum normal-attack distance and how long the attack leaves you exposed.
3. Use `Q` to avoid a committed attack, not simply to travel faster.
4. Save `F` until the target has spent a dash, entered an animation or lost room to escape.
5. Test the `E` gadget separately so you know whether it starts a fight, extends one or helps you leave.
6. Spend early upgrades on the fighter you are actually using before developing the wider roster.

## Tips for winning more fights

**Keep one escape route.** Do not dash into the centre of several opponents unless the move secures an objective or a knockout. Arrive at an angle and keep a path back toward your side.

**Normal attacks create the Super opportunity.** A Super Attack is easiest to dodge when used from neutral space. First pressure the target into moving or spending a dash, then commit the stronger move.

**Do not overlap with teammates.** In team modes, stand close enough to help but not directly on top of an ally. Spacing forces opponents to choose a direction and makes it harder for one area attack to hit the whole group.

**Finish the safe target.** Chasing the lowest-health opponent across the map can open your own team. Take the knockout when it is controlled; otherwise return to the fight that decides the objective.

**Treat trophies as feedback.** A loss streak usually means the current fighter, build or matchup needs practice. Stop queueing long enough to change one thing rather than spending all resources on a random replacement.

## Choosing a mode and role

Community documentation describes 3v3, 1v1 and free-for-all play. In 1v1, matchup knowledge and dash timing decide most exchanges. In free-for-all, survival and positioning matter because another player can interrupt a duel. In 3v3, pressure, space and helping teammates are often more valuable than one extended chase.

Before a match, decide whether the fighter is best at approaching, controlling space or finishing weakened targets. Playing that role consistently makes the abilities easier to understand and gives teammates a predictable partner.
