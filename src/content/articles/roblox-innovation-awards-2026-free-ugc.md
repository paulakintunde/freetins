The Roblox Innovation Awards ceremony on September 12, 2026 had a reward route tied to attending inside The Block. The published event guide associated four base avatar items with featured-game segments. This recap explains that reported setup; it does not promise that entering an afterparty or watching a replay opens the same reward window again.

## The ceremony and the voting hub were different activities

Search results for RIA26 often mix the ceremony with the earlier voting experience. That is an important distinction for anyone trying to identify an item they missed. Voting tasks, ceremony attendance and a later afterparty are not automatically interchangeable claim methods.

The [official The Block listing](https://www.roblox.com/games/95047916580305/The-Block) identifies the experience as Roblox Innovation Studio's event space, published by Live on Roblox. Its identity matters because a similarly named experience is not evidence of an official reward route.

## Which base items the ceremony guide named

[All Things How's ceremony report](https://allthings.how/roblox-innovation-awards-how-to-get-the-free-ria26-ugc-items/) linked the Crown of Rivals to the RIVALS segment, Dueling Defenses to Dueling Grounds, Terminal Torch to Terminal [Escape Room], and Duck Duck Duo to Duck Duck [Tag].

The same report described grants for players present inside The Block during the relevant segments. Watching the broadcast externally was not presented as an equivalent claim method. The account needed to be participating in the Roblox experience, not simply viewing a video of it.

Those are reported event mechanics, rather than a Freetins account's recorded claim results. The distinction is useful when the question is whether a particular account actually received every item.

## What a free catalog listing does and does not show

An avatar item appearing in the catalog can help establish its name, creator and appearance. A free price label alone does not explain how an event distributes it, whether it can be claimed directly or whether an attendance condition has already closed.

When checking a remembered reward, inspect the exact item and creator. Similar names, recolors and copies can make a search result look more relevant than it is. An item image is a reference for identification, not proof that a different experience can grant it.

## Check your inventory before following a recovery claim

Look through the avatar accessories on the account that attended and match the exact item name. If you have multiple Roblox accounts, confirm which one was in The Block. A reward on another account is not necessarily a failed grant to the one you are checking.

If an expected item is absent, preserve any event message or screenshot you already have and consult the official experience's announcements or support route. This recap does not establish that a missing grant can be recreated after the ceremony.

Do not trade away an item or share account credentials with someone promising to recover an event reward. That request is a separate transaction or account-access request, not part of the reported attendance mechanic.

## Silver, gold and replay claims need their own evidence

The ceremony guide did not establish silver and gold variants as public rewards from the same base attendance process. A recolor existing in a catalog does not fill that gap. It needs its own eligibility explanation and claim route.

Likewise, an afterparty continuing to run does not demonstrate that all ceremony grants continue indefinitely. Use a specific new announcement if one establishes another opportunity. Replaying the show may help you understand what happened, but it is not evidence that an inventory grant will be repeated.
