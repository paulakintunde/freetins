## Compatibility is the first specification

PC and console accessory pages often put driver size, lighting zones or charging speed before the question that determines whether a product is useful: will it work with the platform, port and controller you own?

A wired headset can be broadly compatible through a 3.5 mm plug while its USB sound features work only on PC. A programmable keypad may require Windows software and offer no console support. A two-bay charging dock can fit one controller generation and fail completely with another that looks similar. Start with the exact model names, then compare features inside the compatible group.

## Wired headsets: choose the signal path first

The four-pole 3.5 mm connector used on many gaming headsets can carry headphones and microphone through a console controller, laptop or compatible mobile device. It is simple and does not need charging. On a desktop PC with separate microphone and headphone jacks, it may need a splitter.

USB-C or USB-A can add software controls, equalization or virtual surround. Those features do not automatically travel to consoles. A headset may include both analog and USB paths, but the maker's platform table should name what each connection supports.

After compatibility, prioritize comfort, microphone intelligibility and replaceable wear parts. Driver size alone does not establish sound quality. Our [mid-range wired gaming headset guide](/gear/pc-and-console/wired-gaming-headset-mid-range/) compares current broad-platform examples and explains which claims matter.

## Gaming keypads: layout beats an exact key count

One-handed keypads move common controls into a compact cluster and may add a thumb pad, profile switching or macros. They are useful for games with repeated keyboard actions, accessibility setups, small desks or players who prefer a dedicated left-hand shape.

The route for our detailed guide retains “24 key” because that is how the planned product was named, but an exact 24-key gaming model is not the strongest buying target today. Current reputable options often provide 25 to 32 inputs, while some true 24-key mechanical boards are designed for point-of-sale or shortcut work rather than games.

Read the [24-key mechanical keypad and alternatives guide](/gear/pc-and-console/mechanical-keypad-24-key/) for the practical checks: switch type, thumb controls, hand fit, onboard memory, software and whether the game permits macros.

## Charging docks: match the controller, not the console family

The official PlayStation DualSense Charging Station names the controllers it supports and charges two without using the PS5's USB ports. Xbox controllers use replaceable cells by default, so third-party docks commonly depend on included rechargeable packs and special battery doors. A missing or incompatible door can make the dock useless even when its cradle looks right.

Check the exact controller model, not just “PS5” or “Xbox.” DualSense Edge, Xbox Elite and third-party pro controllers may have different contact, battery or shape requirements. The [two-controller charging dock guide](/gear/pc-and-console/charging-dock-two-bay/) covers these platform differences and the parts that should be in the box.

## What to inspect before checkout

1. **Exact compatibility list:** Look for the full controller, console or operating-system name on the maker's page.
2. **Connector by platform:** Separate what 3.5 mm, USB-C and USB-A each provide.
3. **Required software:** Check operating-system support, account requirements and whether settings are stored on the accessory.
4. **Replaceable parts:** Ear pads, cables, batteries and battery doors affect the usable life of the product.
5. **Power supply:** Confirm whether a dock includes its adapter or relies on a console USB port or separate charger.
6. **Warranty and returns:** A clear return path matters for comfort and fit, which a specification table cannot settle.

## Current products that set useful baselines

For wired headsets, the HyperX Cloud III currently includes 3.5 mm, USB-C and USB-A routes and names PC, current and previous PlayStation and Xbox consoles, Nintendo Switch, Mac and mobile. The lighter Razer BlackShark V2 X uses a simpler 3.5 mm path and lists platform support around that connection. Neither is the right fit for every head, but both make the connector decision visible.

For keypads, Razer currently lists the 32-input Tartarus V2 and analog-switch Tartarus Pro. These are not exact 24-key mechanical boards, which is precisely why the category should be compared by layout rather than a stale number. For PS5 charging, Sony's own station is the compatibility baseline; alternatives should explain what they change and why.

## Features that deserve less weight

RGB lighting is visible but rarely changes play. Virtual surround can be software that works only on one operating system. “Universal” charging often means a product line has several platform versions, not that one dock accepts every controller. A high key count can be less useful than a well-placed modifier or thumb control.

Spend the budget on fit, a stable connector, understandable controls and parts that can be replaced. Those qualities remain useful after a marketing feature or software preset stops feeling new.
